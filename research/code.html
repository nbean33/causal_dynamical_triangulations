Unified C++ codebase

Summary

A single C++ codebase simulates CDT in two, three and four dimensions. It includes exact kernel tests, exact checkpointing, and a move-by-move comparison with the original Lisp code.

Design

The codebase keeps the design of JorenB's 2d-cdt and 3d-cdt (Brunekreef, Németh and Görlich; MIT licence), described in their methods paper 2310.16744:

  • simplices and vertices live in fixed-size Pools and are referred to by Labels;
  • random move sites are drawn from O(1) Bags that support uniform pick, insert and remove;
  • static Universe and Simulation classes, Observable subclasses, and a key–value config.dat;
  • one Markov chain per process, so many chains run in parallel with no shared state.

The 2D and 3D parts are the upstream codes, kept in their original style, with every change listed and justified. The 4D part is new: a port of the corrected Lisp sampler, written in the same style.

It is written in C++14 with plain Makefiles and no dependencies beyond the standard library (OpenMP is optional, for observables).

Performance

Following profiling, three changes gave a fivefold speed-up without altering the trajectories: stopping star searches once they exceed the largest star a move can have, building face keys without allocation, and reusing move buffers. The 4D code performs 2.8 million proposals per second at \(N_4 \approx 10\text{k}\) on one core, about 6.5 times the rate of the Lisp code. The large-volume validation ran 26 chains of up to \(3.6\times10^{10}\) proposals each.

Reproducibility

  • Every chain is fully determined by its configuration, its seed and its chain number.
  • Checkpoints save the pools, bags, couplings, both random engines and the lengths of all output files, so a restarted run is bit-for-bit identical to an uninterrupted one. During the validation runs the container runtime stopped once, and every chain resumed exactly from its checkpoint.
  • Observables use a separate random engine, so measurements do not affect the chain.
  • make test runs the baselines, kernel tests, stress tests and checkpoint tests.
