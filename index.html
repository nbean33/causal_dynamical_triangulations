Lattice quantum gravity

Causal Dynamical Triangulations

Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDT) is a non-perturbative approach to quantum gravity. It defines the gravitational path integral as a sum over spacetimes assembled from simplices, with a fixed causal structure.

This site introduces the method and documents a body of simulation work: an audit of existing CDT codes, which found errors in their Monte Carlo samplers; a corrected codebase for two, three and four dimensions; and a comparison of its results with the published literature.

Introduction Research

Summary

Spacetime is approximated by identical building blocks, each spanning one step between neighbouring time slices. A Markov chain samples these configurations with weights given by the discretised Einstein–Hilbert action. In four dimensions, one region of the coupling space produces an extended universe whose average shape matches Euclidean de Sitter space; its spectral dimension is close to four at large scales and decreases towards two at short scales.

Main findings

  • Legacy 4D sampler

    The legacy 4D code constructs valid geometries but uses incorrect acceptance probabilities. For the (2,8)/(8,2) moves alone, the effect is equivalent to shifting \(\kappa_0\) by \(-\ln 4\).

  • Upstream 2D and 3D codes

    An exact test of the acceptance rules found four errors in the 2D and 3D reference codes. The 3D error corresponds to simulating at \(k_0 - \ln 2\).

  • Validation

    The corrected code reproduces the published de Sitter profile, phase structure, A–C transition and spectral dimension.
